::### What PHMSA Data Reveals About Pipeline Failures

Pipelines are critical infrastructure, carrying the energy that powers modern life. But like all infrastructure, they fail. The Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) tracks incidents, leaks, and failures across the U.S. pipeline network. By analyzing these records, we can see which causes dominate and where prevention efforts may have the biggest impact.

The two charts above tell that story.


### Equipment Issues Dominate Failures
The stacked bar chart shows the breakdown of incidents, leaks, and failures by cause. One factor stands far above the rest: **equipment-related issues**. With more than 1,200 recorded cases, equipment problems account for the overwhelming majority of failures.

In contrast, other causes like construction errors, corrosion, and third-party damage fall in the low hundreds. Weather events, incorrect operations, internal corrosion, and stress cracking barely register by comparison.

This highlights a critical truth. Most pipeline risk is not from external shocks but from the mechanical systems themselves --- valves, pumps, joints, and fittings that degrade or malfunction over time.

### Proportions Show Where to Focus
The pie chart makes the picture even clearer. **Nearly two-thirds (62.5%) of all failures come from equipment**. The next largest categories, **construction issues (9.9%)** and **external corrosion (9.0%)**, are significant but much smaller.

Other categories --- third-party damage, manufacturing defects, incorrect operations, and environmental forces --- make up only small slices of the total.

From a risk management perspective, this suggests that targeting equipment reliability could yield the biggest payoff in reducing incidents.

### What This Means for Pipeline Operators
These findings point to a few important takeaways:

1.  [**Maintenance is king**. Equipment dominates the risk profile, so predictive maintenance and inspection programs should be central to safety strategies.]
2.  [**Construction quality matters**. Nearly 10% of issues trace back to construction. Better oversight during build phases can prevent long-term problems.]
3.  [**Corrosion is still a threat**. External corrosion remains a leading cause, underlining the importance of coatings, cathodic protection, and regular monitoring.]
4.  [**Technology can help**. Advanced sensors, SCADA integration, and AI-driven leak detection can catch equipment issues earlier and reduce downtime.]

### So what?
PHMSA data makes one thing clear: the weakest link in the pipeline system is often the equipment itself. While dramatic failures from weather or outside damage capture headlines, the daily grind of valves, compressors, and fittings poses the greater risk. By focusing on equipment reliability, operators can reduce the majority of leaks and failures, improving both safety and efficiency. ::
